About

Design, manufacturing, and materials, end to end.

I am a mechanical engineer and fourth year PhD candidate at the University of Texas at Arlington. My doctoral work builds enhanced printheads and designed experiments that solve the oldest problem in 3D printing, that parts are weak in the direction they are built. My background, though, is broader than additive manufacturing.

Before the PhD I spent four years in industry as a design engineer and in business development. I designed structural components and piping for a 45,300 cubic meter fuel storage terminal to API 650, engineered NFPA 11 fire protection systems, and developed 3D printed hardware for solar microgrids. That work built deep competence in mechanical design, CAD, and design for manufacturing and assembly. My review paper on design for additive manufacturing of lightweight structures brings the same discipline to printed parts.

What ties it together is measurement. I read a part through a full characterization stack: SEM and micro CT for structure and porosity, DSC and STA for thermal behavior, FTIR and Raman for chemistry, and FLIR thermography, DIC, and acoustic emission during the process itself. I turn that data into physics based and machine learning models. Whatever the part, I can show why it behaves the way it does and use that to make the next one better.

Doctoral Research

Fixing the weak link between layers.

The weak spot in a 3D printed part is the bond between layers. Earlier work only theorized the crystallization and diffusion competition in semicrystalline polymers that governs that bond. My research proves it and puts it to work. By modifying G code to add programmed interlayer cooling alongside in process annealing, I control that competition directly.

A full factorial experiment on a custom heated plate printhead raised interlayer strength to about 86 percent of bulk PLA on average, with the best replicate near 92 percent, while cutting weld line porosity. DSC isolated crystallization arrested healing as the controlling mechanism, and I built coupled healing and porosity models in Python to predict bond development from a part's measured thermal history. I am now on the second generation printhead, a modular design that cuts the custom part count from five to three and integrates stock heating elements for reliability.
